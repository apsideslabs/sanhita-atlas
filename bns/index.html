The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023

The general penal code of India from 1 July 2024. It consolidates and amends the law relating to offences, replacing the Indian Penal Code of 1860 — the statute that had governed Indian criminal law for over 160 years.

In force 1 July 2024Act No. 45 of 2023358 sections20 chapters

The Sanhita received assent on 25 December 2023 and came into force on 1 July 2024, together with the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (procedure) and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (evidence). The three together replace the IPC, the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 and the Indian Evidence Act, 1872.

Structurally the Sanhita follows the IPC: definitions, then punishments, then general exceptions, then the substantive offences grouped by subject. What changed is the numbering, the grouping, and a set of new offences aimed at organised crime, terrorism and offences committed by groups.

What the Sanhita does differently

Numbering

Renumbered, not reorganised

The code runs to 358 sections against the IPC's 511. Much of the substance is carried across, but almost every section number moved — which is why old judgments and new FIRs no longer speak the same language.

New offences

Organised crime and terrorism

Sections 111, 112 and 113 create standalone offences of organised crime, petty organised crime and terrorist acts — categories the IPC dealt with only indirectly, through conspiracy and unlawful assembly.

Sentencing

Community service enters the code

Section 4 adds community service as a seventh punishment, available for six specified offences including small-value theft and defamation.

Language

Sedition recast

Section 124-A of the IPC does not survive. Section 152 punishes acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity — exciting secession or armed rebellion, or encouraging such feelings.

Reading the section ranges

Chapter ranges in this platform follow the Act as notified, not the Bill as introduced. The Bill had 356 clauses; two sections were added during passage, which shifts numbering in the later chapters.
