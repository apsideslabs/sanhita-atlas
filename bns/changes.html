What changed on 1 July 2024

The Sanhita is not a rewrite of the IPC so much as a renumbering with a set of deliberate changes layered on top. These are the ones that alter outcomes rather than drafting style.

Offences with no IPC equivalent

Nine of the most consequential additions. Several respond to conduct the courts had been struggling to fit into conspiracy, unlawful assembly or abetment.

Section 111

Organised crime

Continuing unlawful activity by a crime syndicate — kidnapping, extortion, land grabbing, contract killing, financial fraud, trafficking, cyber-crime and more. Punishable with death or life where it results in death.

Section 112

Petty organised crime

The smaller-scale counterpart: theft, snatching, cheating, unauthorised betting or gambling committed by a syndicate. Up to seven years.

Section 113

Terrorist act

A standalone offence of doing an act intended to threaten the unity, integrity, sovereignty or security of India, or to strike terror in the people. Death or life where it causes death.

Section 101(2)

Group murder on identity grounds

Murder committed by a group of five or more on grounds of race, caste, community, sex, place of birth or language — the provision aimed at mob lynching. Death or life and a fine of at least ₹5 lakh.

Section 106(2)

Fleeing after a fatal accident

A driver who causes death by rash or negligent driving and then escapes without reporting it faces up to ten years and a fine.

Section 304

Snatching

Sudden seizure of movable property from a person or their possession. Up to three years on a first conviction.

Section 69

Sexual intercourse by deceitful means

Obtaining intercourse by deceitful means or by a false promise of marriage, where the act does not amount to rape.

Section 152

Acts endangering sovereignty

Not strictly new — it replaces sedition — but the offence is redrawn around secession, armed rebellion and the encouragement of such feelings.

Section 226

Attempt to suicide

Retained as an offence but reconceived: a presumption of severe stress, and a duty on the State to provide rehabilitation rather than punishment.

Provisions left behind

IPCWhat happened
IPC 309Attempt to suicide — retained in form but converted into a rehabilitation provision.
IPC 310 / 311The offence of being a 'thug' and its punishment — obsolete and removed.
IPC 497Adultery — struck down by the Supreme Court in Joseph Shine (2018) and not carried into the Sanhita.
IPC 124ASedition as a label — replaced by Section 152.
IPC 153ACommunal enmity — recast, with 'language' added to the protected grounds.

Penalties that moved

Three changes matter most in practice.

Death by negligence

Section 106

The base punishment rose from two years to five. A new sub-section (2) punishes a driver who causes death by rash driving and then flees without reporting — up to ten years and a fine.

Organised crime

Sections 111 and 112

A crime syndicate's continuing unlawful activity is now an offence in itself, with death or life where it results in death, and a minimum of five years otherwise.

Community service

Section 4

Added as a seventh punishment, available for six offences including small-value theft, defamation, misconduct in public by a drunken person and attempt to suicide.
