Section index

A working index of the sections that carry the most weight in practice — the ones cited in chargesheets, judgments and commentary. Filter by chapter or search by offence.

94 sections indexedOut of 358
SectionTitle ChapterWhat it covers
1Short title, commencement and applicationCh INames the Sanhita, brings it into force on 1 July 2024 and fixes its territorial reach, including offences on ships and aircraft.
2DefinitionsCh IThirty-nine definitions that carry the whole code — 'act', 'document', 'public servant', 'wrongful gain' and the rest.
3General explanationsCh IRules of construction: how to read references to acts, omissions, intention and common intention.
4PunishmentsCh IIThe seven punishments: death, imprisonment for life, rigorous imprisonment, simple imprisonment, forfeiture, fine and community service.
5Commutation of sentenceCh IIThe appropriate Government may commute a death sentence and, in certain cases, imprisonment for life.
6Fractions of terms of punishmentCh IIHow fractions of a term are computed where the code refers to a portion of the maximum sentence.
8Amount of fine and liability in defaultCh IIWhat a court must consider when fixing a fine, and the imprisonment that follows non-payment.
11Solitary confinementCh IIWhen solitary confinement may be imposed, and the categories of prisoner excluded from it.
13Enhanced punishment after previous convictionCh IIHigher sentences for repeat offenders in the specified cases.
14Act done by a person bound by lawCh IIINo offence where the act is done by a person bound by law, or who by mistake of fact believes himself bound.
20Act of a child under seven yearsCh IIINothing done by a child under seven is an offence.
22Act of a person of unsound mindCh IIIProtects a person incapable of knowing the nature or wrongness of the act by reason of unsoundness of mind.
25Act done by consentCh IIIConsent removes criminality where the act is not intended and not known to be likely to cause death or grievous hurt.
33Act causing slight harmCh IIITrifling harm is not an offence — the statutory de minimis principle.
34Things done in private defenceCh IIINothing is an offence that is done in the exercise of the right of private defence.
38Private defence extending to causing deathCh IIIThe situations in which the right of private defence of the body extends to causing death.
45Abetment of a thingCh IVAbetment by instigation, by conspiracy and by intentional aid.
61Criminal conspiracyCh IVAgreement to commit an offence, or to do an illegal act by illegal means; punishment in sub-section (2).
62Attempt to commit offencesCh IVThe general punishment for attempting an offence punishable with imprisonment for life or other imprisonment.
63Rape — definitionCh VDefines rape across seven clauses, and retains the marital exception for a wife not under eighteen.
64Punishment for rapeCh VRigorous imprisonment of not less than ten years, extendable to life, and a fine.
65Rape causing death or persistent vegetative stateCh VRigorous imprisonment of not less than twenty years, extending to life, or death.
66Rape of a child under twelveCh VRigorous imprisonment of not less than twenty years, extending to life, or death.
69Sexual intercourse by employing deceitful meansCh VA new offence: obtaining intercourse by deceitful means or by a false promise of marriage.
70Gang rapeCh VRigorous imprisonment of not less than twenty years, extending to life, and a fine.
74Assault on a woman with intent to outrage modestyCh VAssault or criminal force on a woman with intent to outrage her modesty.
75Sexual harassmentCh VUnwelcome physical contact, demands for sexual favours, showing pornography and sexually coloured remarks.
76Assault with intent to disrobeCh VAssault or criminal force on a woman with intent to disrobe her.
77VoyeurismCh VWatching or capturing images of a woman in a private act without consent.
78StalkingCh VFollowing a woman, contacting her against her wishes, or monitoring her use of the internet.
79Insult to the modesty of a womanCh VUttering any word or making any gesture intended to insult the modesty of a woman.
80Dowry deathCh VDeath of a woman within seven years of marriage where there was cruelty or harassment for dowry.
81Cohabitation caused by deceitful meansCh VInducing a woman to believe she is lawfully married and causing her to cohabit.
85Cruelty by husband or relativesCh VThe offence formerly in Section 498-A IPC, with its explanation of mental and physical cruelty.
86Causing miscarriageCh VVoluntarily causing a woman with child to miscarry, with aggravated forms.
94Sale of a minor for prostitutionCh VSelling, letting to hire or otherwise disposing of a minor for prostitution.
100Culpable homicideCh VIDefines culpable homicide as causing death by doing an act with the intention or knowledge specified in the section.
101MurderCh VIDefines murder, and sub-section (2) covers group killing on grounds of race, caste, community, sex, place of birth or language.
103Punishment for murderCh VIDeath or imprisonment for life, and a fine.
105Punishment for culpable homicide not amounting to murderCh VIImprisonment for life, or imprisonment of either description up to ten years, with fine.
106Causing death by negligenceCh VIUp to five years and fine; sub-section (2) covers a driver who flees after a fatal accident — up to ten years.
108Abetment of suicideCh VIAbetting the commission of suicide, with a heavier punishment where a child or a person of unsound mind is abetted.
109Attempt to murderCh VIAttempt to murder, with a higher punishment where hurt is caused.
111Organised crimeCh VIA new offence covering continuing unlawful activity by a crime syndicate — kidnapping, extortion, land grabbing, contract killing, cyber-crime and more.
112Petty organised crimeCh VIThe smaller-scale counterpart to Section 111: theft, snatching, cheating and similar acts by a syndicate.
113Terrorist actCh VIA new standalone offence of doing an act intended to threaten the unity, integrity, sovereignty or security of India.
115Voluntarily causing hurtCh VIHurt, defined as bodily pain, disease or infirmity; punishment in sub-section (2).
117Voluntarily causing grievous hurtCh VIThe eight categories of grievous hurt, and the punishment for causing it.
118Hurt by dangerous weaponsCh VIVoluntarily causing hurt by means of an instrument for shooting, stabbing or cutting.
124Hurt by acid attackCh VIVoluntarily causing hurt by throwing acid, with a minimum sentence of ten years.
126Wrongful restraintCh VIVoluntarily obstructing a person so as to prevent movement in a direction in which he has a right to proceed.
127Wrongful confinementCh VIWrongfully restraining a person so as to prevent movement beyond certain limits.
137KidnappingCh VIKidnapping from India and kidnapping from lawful guardianship, and the punishment for kidnapping.
138AbductionCh VICompelling a person by force or deceit to go from one place to another.
140Kidnapping for ransomCh VIKidnapping or abduction in order to murder, or for ransom — punishable with death or imprisonment for life.
143Trafficking of a personCh VITrafficking for exploitation, with graded punishments by age of the victim and number of victims.
146Unlawful compulsory labourCh VICompelling a person to labour against his will.
147Waging war against IndiaCh VIIWaging, attempting to wage or abetting war against the Government of India.
152Acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of IndiaCh VIIThe provision that replaced sedition — intentionally exciting secession or armed rebellion, or encouraging such feelings.
189Unlawful assemblyCh XIDefines an assembly of five or more persons with a common object falling within the listed clauses.
191RiotingCh XIThe use of force or violence by an unlawful assembly, and the punishment for rioting.
196Promoting enmity between groupsCh XIPromoting disharmony or feelings of enmity on grounds of religion, race, place of birth, residence or language.
198Public servant disobeying the lawCh XIIA public servant knowingly disobeying a direction of law with intent to cause injury.
226Attempt to commit suicideCh XIIIAttempt to suicide, with a presumption of severe stress and a direction towards rehabilitation rather than imprisonment.
229False evidenceCh XIVGiving false evidence, and fabricating false evidence, in a judicial proceeding.
238Causing disappearance of evidenceCh XIVCausing disappearance of evidence of an offence, or giving false information to screen an offender.
270Public nuisanceCh XVAn act or illegal omission causing common injury, danger or annoyance to the public.
294Obscene acts and songsCh XVObscene acts in a public place, and obscene songs, ballads or words.
298Injuring or defiling a place of worshipCh XVIDestroying, damaging or defiling a place of worship with intent to insult the religion of any class.
299Outraging religious feelingsCh XVIDeliberate and malicious acts intended to outrage the religious feelings of any class.
303TheftCh XVIIDishonest taking of movable property out of another's possession without consent; punishment in sub-section (2).
304SnatchingCh XVIIA new offence: sudden seizure of movable property from a person or their possession.
305Theft in a dwelling houseCh XVIIAggravated theft in a dwelling house, means of transportation or place of worship.
308ExtortionCh XVIIIntentionally putting a person in fear of injury in order to dishonestly induce the delivery of property.
309RobberyCh XVIITheft or extortion aggravated by force or fear of instant hurt.
310DacoityCh XVIIRobbery committed by five or more persons.
314Dishonest misappropriation of propertyCh XVIIConverting another's property to one's own use dishonestly.
316Criminal breach of trustCh XVIIDishonest misappropriation of property entrusted to a person, with aggravated forms for carriers, clerks and public servants.
317Receiving stolen propertyCh XVIIDishonestly receiving or retaining property knowing it to be stolen.
318CheatingCh XVIIDeceiving a person to dishonestly induce delivery of property, with graded punishments by value and consequence.
319Cheating by personationCh XVIICheating by pretending to be some other person.
324MischiefCh XVIICausing wrongful loss or damage by destroying or diminishing the value of property.
329Criminal trespass and house-trespassCh XVIIEntry into property in the possession of another with intent to commit an offence or to intimidate.
330House-trespass and house-breakingCh XVIIThe aggravated forms of trespass, including breaking into a house.
335Making a false documentCh XVIIIDefines what it means to make a false document or electronic record.
336ForgeryCh XVIIIMaking a false document with intent to cause injury, and the punishment for forgery.
338Forgery of a valuable security or willCh XVIIIAggravated forgery of a valuable security, will or authority to adopt.
340Forged document used as genuineCh XVIIIUsing a forged document or electronic record as genuine, knowing it to be forged.
344Falsification of accountsCh XVIIIDestroying, altering or falsifying accounts with intent to defraud.
351Criminal intimidationCh XIXThreatening injury to a person, reputation or property with intent to cause alarm or to compel an act.
352Intentional insultCh XIXIntentional insult with intent to provoke a breach of the peace.
353Statements conducing to public mischiefCh XIXPublishing statements likely to cause mutiny, desertion or fear, or to incite offences against the State.
356DefamationCh XIXDefamation across four sub-sections, including printing and selling defamatory matter, with community service as an alternative to imprisonment.
358Repeal and savingsCh XXRepeals the Indian Penal Code, 1860, and saves pending proceedings under the old law.

This is a curated index of 94 sections, not the full text of all 358. It covers the provisions that account for most practical work; the chapter pages give the complete range.
