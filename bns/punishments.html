The punishment framework

Section 4 lists seven punishments. The Sanhita keeps the IPC's structure but adds community service, and introduces statutory minimums for a set of offences that previously had none.

7 punishments23 minimum sentencesCommunity service added

The seven punishments — Section 4

01

Death

Awarded for a defined set of offences — murder, gang rape in certain cases, terrorism, and repeat offences. It is no longer mandatory in any case; the court must record reasons where it chooses life instead.

02

Imprisonment for life

The default alternative to death. Its meaning is fixed by the procedure code, and commutation is governed by Section 5.

03

Rigorous imprisonment

Imprisonment with hard labour. Used where the section specifies 'rigorous'.

04

Simple imprisonment

Imprisonment without hard labour, for the offences the code marks as simple.

05

Forfeiture of property

Available only where the section expressly provides for it.

06

Fine

Fixed or discretionary, with the considerations in Section 8 and imprisonment in default.

07

Community service

New in 2023. Ordered for six specified offences, and conceived as an alternative to short custodial sentences.

How the sentence is computed

SectionProvisionEffect
5Commutation The appropriate Government may commute a death sentence, and may commute life imprisonment in the cases listed — for example where the offender is a woman or a person under eighteen.
6Fractions of a term Where the code refers to a portion of a maximum term, the portion is calculated on that maximum.
8Fine and default Fixing a fine turns on the offender's means and the injury caused; non-payment converts to imprisonment within statutory limits.
9–10Several offences The limit on punishment where an act constitutes several offences, and the rule where the judgment is doubtful as to which offence was committed.
11–12Solitary confinement Permitted only in the listed cases, and barred for women and for persons sentenced to simple imprisonment.
13Repeat offenders Enhanced punishment after a previous conviction in the specified cases.

Minimum sentences

Twenty-three offences acquired a statutory floor. The ones most often encountered:

SectionOffenceMinimum
64RapeTen years rigorous imprisonment, extendable to life
65Rape causing death or a persistent vegetative stateTwenty years, extendable to life
66Rape of a child under twelveTwenty years, extendable to life, or death
70Gang rapeTwenty years, extendable to life
103MurderDeath or life, and a fine
111Organised crimeFive years; death or life where it causes death
113Terrorist actFive years; death or life where it causes death
124Acid attackTen years, extendable to life

Community service in practice

Section 4(f) makes community service a punishment, but the code does not define it. The procedure code and the rules made under it supply the mechanics — the court orders the work, and non-compliance is treated as a breach of the order.
