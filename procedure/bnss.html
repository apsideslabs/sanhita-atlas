Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023

The procedure code that replaced the CrPC on 1 July 2024. It governs arrest, investigation, bail and trial — and it is where most of the practical change for a client actually sits.

In force 1 July 2024Act No. 46 of 2023

The BNSS is the companion statute to the Sanhita: the Sanhita defines the offence, the BNSS supplies the machinery. Its stated aim is time-bound investigation and trial, and much of the drafting is directed at delay rather than at substantive rights.

The anchors that matter

SectionProvisionWhat it does
173FIR, including a Zero FIRInformation in a cognizable case is recorded electronically or otherwise; a Zero FIR can be lodged at any police station regardless of jurisdiction, and an e-FIR is permitted for specified offences.
173(3)Preliminary enquiryFor offences punishable between three and seven years, a police officer may conduct a preliminary enquiry within fourteen days to decide whether a prima facie case exists.
35ArrestArrest without warrant, with the notice-of-appearance regime for offences punishable with less than seven years, and the requirement to display the officer's identity.
187RemandProduction before a magistrate within twenty-four hours and the authorisation of further detention during investigation.
193Report on completion of investigationThe charge sheet, with a mandatory timeline for completing the investigation in specified cases.
480Bail in non-bailable offencesThe discretionary bail power, with special protection for a person under trial who has served half the maximum sentence.
483Special powers of the High Court and Sessions CourtThe superior courts' power to grant bail, and to direct that a person be released on a bond.

What changes for a client

Filing

Zero FIR and e-FIR

A first information report can be filed at any police station regardless of where the offence happened, and transmitted to the station with jurisdiction. Electronic filing is expressly permitted.

Arrest

Notice instead of arrest

For offences punishable with less than seven years, the officer must ordinarily issue a notice of appearance rather than arrest — codifying the Arnesh Kumar guidelines.

Bail

Half the sentence served

An undertrial who has served half the maximum sentence for a non-bailable offence is entitled to be released on bond, subject to the exceptions in the section.

Verify before relying

The BNSS has been amended and supplemented by rules and notifications since commencement, and several provisions are being implemented in phases. Check the current text on India Code.
