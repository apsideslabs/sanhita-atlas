Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023

The evidence code that replaced the Indian Evidence Act, 1872. Its most consequential change is the treatment of electronic records as primary evidence.

In force 1 July 2024Act No. 47 of 2023

The BSA keeps the architecture of the 1872 Act — relevance, admissibility, proof, and the examination of witnesses — and rewrites it for a document environment that is now largely digital. The provisions that litigators actually argue about are the ones dealing with electronic records.

Electronic records

SectionProvisionWhat it does
63Electronic recordsElectronic records now stand on the same footing as documents. A certificate is required where the record is produced by a device, and the conditions formerly in Section 65-B of the Evidence Act are carried forward.
57Primary evidenceElectronic records are treated as primary evidence, narrowing the gap between paper and data.
63(4)PresumptionsWhere an electronic record is produced with the required certificate, the court may presume the device was working properly and the record is authentic.

Why it matters

Before

Section 65-B certificates

Under the 1872 Act an electronic record was secondary evidence, admissible only with a certificate identifying the device, the custodian and the process. Disputes over the certificate were a routine source of acquittals.

After

Primary evidence

The BSA treats the electronic record itself as primary evidence, while retaining a certificate requirement where the record is produced by a device. The practical effect is to shift argument from admissibility to authenticity.

Read the section, not the summary

The certificate requirement is the most litigated provision in the new code. The wording of Section 63 and the form of the certificate in the Schedule govern whether a record is admitted.
