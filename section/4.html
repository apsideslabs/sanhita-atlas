4Chapter II

Punishments

Section 4AnnotatedIPC 53In force 1 Jul 2024

The punishments to which offenders are liable under the provisions of this Sanhita are—

(a) Death;

(b) Imprisonment for life;

(c) Imprisonment, which is of two descriptions, namely:—

(1) Rigorous, that is, with hard labour;

(2) Simple;

(d) Forfeiture of property;

(e) Fine;

(f) Community Service.

What it means

Section 4 fixes the menu of sentences a court may impose. Six are carried over from the IPC; the seventh — community service — is new, and it is the only punishment in the code that is defined nowhere else.

The order matters in practice: a court must select from this list, and a sentence that is not one of the seven is not a sentence the Sanhita authorises.

Worked example

Illustration

An offender convicted for the first time of theft of property worth less than ₹5,000 may be ordered to perform community service instead of a short custodial sentence, under the proviso to s. 303(2).

Case law

Bachan Singh v State of Punjab

(1980) 2 SCC 684

Death is a permissible but exceptional sentence. A court must weigh aggravating against mitigating circumstances and record reasons; it cannot be imposed as a matter of course.

Mithu v State of Punjab

(1983) 2 SCC 277

A provision making the death sentence mandatory for a class of murders was struck down — the sentencing discretion cannot be taken away by statute.
