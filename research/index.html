Methodology and sources

How this platform was compiled, what it covers, and where its limits are. Every section number here traces to the Act as notified, not to secondary commentary.

Compiled October 20266 primary sources

What this is

Sanhita Atlas indexes the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 and its two companion codes. It is built for people who need to find a provision quickly — a section number, the offence it creates, its punishment, and the IPC section it corresponds to — and for people trying to understand what actually changed on 1 July 2024.

It is deliberately not a commentary. There is no case law digest and no editorial opinion on how a provision should be read; the judgments that interpret the Sanhita are still being written.

Editorial rules

RuleWhat it means in practice
Numbers come from the Act Section and chapter ranges follow the Act as notified on 25 December 2023, not the Bill as introduced. Two sections were added during passage, which shifts numbering in the later chapters.
Mappings are selective The IPC–BNS table covers the provisions most often looked up. Where a provision was substantively rewritten, the note says so rather than implying equivalence.
No inference of punishment Where a punishment is not stated in a summary, it is because the section was not verified against the text — not because the offence is unpunished.
Currency The platform reflects the position as at 29 October 2026. Amendments after that date are not reflected.

Known limits

This is an index, not the statute

The section index covers 94 of the 358 sections — the ones that carry most practical weight. The full text of every provision is available on India Code and in the Gazette notification. Nothing here is legal advice.
